# CO2 exploratory models v0.1 — specification fixed before fitting

Input: analytic_sample_v01/output/observaciones_principal.csv, analytic sample v0.1 / base v0.2.2. Previous data, code and manuscript remain unchanged. Universe: expanded collaboration network recovered from an entrepreneurial marketing corpus; membership is not thematic membership and first authorship is not demonstrated leadership.

Main: outcome years <=2025. Sensitivities: joint quality exclusion already flagged in input, years <=2025; full sample including one 2026 outcome. No selection on outcome changes or indicator variation. Windows [t-3,t-1] unchanged. All single-context investigators retained.

Outcome: signing_role first=1, nonfirst=0 among eligible coauthored outcomes. Prior category: exclusively_nonfirst_observed (reference), exclusively_first_observed, mixed. Controls fixed a priori: log2(number of prior coauthored publications with identified partner); log2(n_authors_evidence_max) (largest recovered documentary team-size evidence, not necessarily a fully verified team); calendar (outcome_year-2010)/10. Team size is contextual adjustment, not a proven causal control. No automatic selection, splines, extra indicators or within-person decomposition in this round.

M0: prior category + controls + Normal(0,sigma^2) investigator intercept.
H1: M0 + D + D*prior category, D=(alter_density-0.5)/0.1. H1 requires positive density slope in exclusively-first AND negative slope in exclusively-nonfirst. Mixed slope separate.
H2: M0 + C, C=(burt_constraint_weighted-0.5)/0.1. Expected slope negative. H1 and H2 fitted separately.

Frequentist marginal maximum likelihood using 60-point Gauss-Hermite quadrature, analytical gradient, L-BFGS-B; sigma bounds exp(-7) to exp(3), diagnostic rather than silently treating boundaries as interior. Check 100-point refit and multiple starting sigma values on original fits. No publication random intercept: such a term would not enforce one first author per paper and could misrepresent the negative dependence among its outcomes.

Uncertainty: 399 two-way pigeonhole bootstrap replicates per dataset/model, PCG64 seed 20261030. Independently resample investigator IDs and publication IDs with multinomial multiplicities. For investigator i, weight its integrated log likelihood by its investigator multiplicity; weight each Bernoulli contribution inside that integral by its publication multiplicity. Duplicate investigators thus have separate identical likelihood contributions, not a single shared intercept across copies. This preserves observed outcomes and clusters repeated/overlapping-window records of a researcher together, while resampling shared publication contributions jointly. No individual-row bootstrap or publication-intercept correction. Report percentile 95% intervals, successful/failed/boundary replicates and finite-bootstrap limits. Refit original failures with alternate starts; no case deletion or hypothesis reformulation. Bootstrap maximum-likelihood boundary sigma is permitted if gradient satisfies bounded optimum. Failed draws are retained in diagnostics, not replaced.

This bootstrap is motivated by crossed-array resampling (Owen, 2007; Owen & Eckles, 2012). Their mean/random-array results do not establish exact coverage for this nonlinear, network-derived GLMM. It is an exploratory robustness approximation, may be conservative in sparse crossed arrays, does not reproduce dependencies through unobserved coauthors, and is not guaranteed to cover every dependency due to shared prior network documents across investigators. Inference remains provisional. Bootstrap does not repair bias of the independent-researcher working likelihood when publication dependence or missingness is informative. No p-value-based hypothesis decisions.

Predictions: integrate over a new investigator's Normal intercept (not fitted empirical individual intercepts), then standardize over observed outcome rows within each prior category. Density curves/contrasts only within that category's observed density range; contrast main-sample category-specific quartiles, retaining same endpoints in sensitivity if inside its range. H2 overall contrast low to high constraint using main pooled quartiles, conditional on empirical prior category and covariates; additional category-specific predictions. Predictions need not sum to one across coauthors and are not a model of article-level first-author allocation. No causal or exclusively within-person interpretation. Identical bootstrap draws paired across models within dataset. Coefficients, ORs, probability differences and bootstrap intervals reported.

Sources: Owen, A. B. (2007). The pigeonhole bootstrap. The Annals of Applied Statistics, 1(2), 386–411. https://doi.org/10.1214/07-AOAS122
Owen, A. B., & Eckles, D. (2012). Bootstrapping data arrays of arbitrary order. The Annals of Applied Statistics, 6(3), 895–927. https://doi.org/10.1214/12-AOAS547

## Numerical amendment during bootstrap (before interpreting intervals)

High-sigma bootstrap replicates exposed insufficient accuracy of ordinary 60-node quadrature. All 399 draws per specification are therefore recomputed using adaptive Gauss-Hermite quadrature (80 nodes, checked at 120; escalate to 120/180 and check at 180/260 when absolute log-likelihood difference exceeds 1e-4). A numerical acceptance limit of 1e-3 for check difference applies. This changes integration accuracy only, not variables, samples, controls, hypotheses, resampling seeds or estimands. Original fits remain accurate against 100 nodes. The discarded preliminary nonadaptive bootstrap is not used for reported intervals.
